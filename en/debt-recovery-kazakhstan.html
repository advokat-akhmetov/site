Debt Disputes

Winning a court case and actually recovering the money are not the same thing

An effective recovery strategy should consider not only the legal claim itself, but also the available evidence, the debtor's financial position, the risk of asset disposal and the practical enforcement of a future judgment.

Debt may arise from a supply agreement, construction contract, services agreement, loan, advance payment, unpaid invoice or other contractual obligation. In business disputes, the contract alone is rarely the entire case: delivery documents, acceptance certificates, payments, correspondence and the conduct of the parties may all be important.

Practical approach: before starting litigation, it is useful to answer two separate questions: whether the creditor has a legally supportable claim and how a favorable judgment could ultimately be enforced.

Before Litigation

What should be reviewed before starting debt recovery

Court proceedings should be preceded by a review of the legal basis of the claim, evidence, jurisdiction and practical enforcement risks.

Legal Basis

What agreement or legal relationship created the obligation, and whether the debtor's payment or performance obligation is due.

Evidence

Contracts, invoices, delivery documents, acceptance certificates, bank records, emails, messages, demands and other evidence.

Amount of the Claim

Principal debt and any additional claims should be calculated separately and supported by an appropriate legal and documentary basis.

Pre-Action Procedure

It should be established whether the contract or applicable law requires a demand or other pre-action procedure before filing the claim.

Jurisdiction

The competent court must be determined with regard to the parties, contractual provisions and the circumstances of the dispute.

Debtor's Assets

A recovery strategy should consider whether the debtor has bank funds, property or other assets against which enforcement may be possible.

Amount Claimed

What may form part of a debt recovery claim

The recoverable components depend on the contract, applicable law and the circumstances of the particular dispute.

Principal Debt

The first step is to establish the amount of the outstanding monetary obligation and the evidence supporting it.

Contractual Penalties

Where legally justified, contractual or statutory penalties may be considered as part of the claim. Their basis and amount require separate analysis.

Damages

If the breach caused financial loss, the legal basis, amount of the loss and causal connection must be assessed and supported.

Use of Another's Money

In circumstances provided for by Kazakhstan civil law, additional liability associated with the unlawful use of another person's money may be considered.

Important: additional amounts should not simply be added to the principal debt. Each component of the claim requires its own legal and factual basis.
Asset Risk

Interim measures and preservation of enforcement prospects

If there are circumstances indicating that enforcement of a future judgment may become difficult or impossible, the creditor should assess whether there are legal grounds to request interim measures.

The appropriate measure depends on the subject matter, the amount claimed, the relevant assets and the circumstances of the case. The decision whether to grant interim measures is made by the court.

After Judgment

Enforcement is a separate stage of debt recovery

A favorable judgment establishes the creditor's legal position, but actual recovery may require further work during enforcement proceedings.

A judgment is not the same as money in the bank

After obtaining the relevant enforcement document, the creditor proceeds to the enforcement stage and may need to monitor the practical progress of recovery.

Information concerning bank funds, property and other assets of the debtor may become particularly important at this stage.

Enforcement Support

Legal assistance may include reviewing the status of enforcement proceedings, preparing applications and submissions, and protecting the creditor's interests during the enforcement process.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions about debt recovery in Kazakhstan

Can a foreign company recover a debt from a company in Kazakhstan?

A foreign creditor may pursue recovery where there is a proper legal basis. The contract, jurisdiction, applicable law, evidence and enforcement strategy should be reviewed before proceedings begin.

What documents are needed for debt recovery?

Relevant documents may include contracts, amendments, invoices, delivery documents, acceptance certificates, bank records, correspondence, formal demands and other evidence showing the obligation and non-performance.

Is a demand required before filing a court claim?

Whether a pre-action procedure is mandatory depends on the applicable law, contractual terms and the nature of the claim. This should be checked before proceedings are commenced.

What if the Kazakhstan debtor is disposing of assets?

Where legally justified, the creditor may consider requesting interim measures. Their availability and proportionality depend on the circumstances, and the decision is made by the court.

Does a court judgment guarantee payment?

No judgment automatically means immediate payment. Enforcement is a separate stage and may require work concerning the debtor's bank funds, property and other assets.

Can Russian, Chinese and other foreign companies seek legal assistance in Kazakhstan?

Foreign businesses and individuals may seek Kazakhstan-side legal assistance. The required documents and representation arrangements depend on the particular dispute and the procedural steps required.
