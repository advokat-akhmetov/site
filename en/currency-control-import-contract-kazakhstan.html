LEGAL REVIEW

Currency control begins with the contract itself

When entering into a contract with a foreign supplier or buyer, the legal analysis should not be limited to the price of the goods, delivery terms and liability provisions.

The terms of a cross-border transaction may directly affect currency-control requirements, performance deadlines and the documents that must be provided to the servicing bank.

A contractual issue that appears minor at the drafting stage may become significant when a payment is made, a contract registration number is required, a delivery deadline changes, an advance payment must be recovered, or performance by the non-resident must be documented.

Practical approach: currency-control provisions should preferably be reviewed before the contract is signed and before performance begins, rather than after a bank or currency-control authority raises questions about the transaction.
> $50,000 registration threshold

When is contract registration required?

Under the applicable export-import currency control rules, an import or export currency contract is subject to registration when the contract amount exceeds the equivalent of USD 50,000.

If the contract amount is not specified on the date the contract is entered into, the currency contract is also treated as being subject to registration.

REGISTRATION NUMBER

Why does a foreign trade contract need a registration number?

Registration identifies a specific import or export currency contract and enables performance of obligations under that contract to be accounted for within the currency-control system.

1

Contract identification

A registration number links the contract with the relevant payments and performance of the parties' obligations.

2

Performance monitoring

The registration bank accounts for information relating to performance of obligations under the registered currency contract.

3

Repatriation period

The contractual terms should make it possible to determine performance deadlines and properly address the applicable currency-control requirements.

4

Contract amendments

Amendments affecting delivery periods, contract value or other material terms should also be assessed from the perspective of currency-control compliance.

PROCESS

How a foreign trade currency contract is handled

1. Contract

The parties agree on the subject matter, price, currency, payment terms, delivery and deadlines for performance.

2. Threshold review

It is determined whether the currency contract is subject to registration.

3. Registration

Where the applicable legal requirements are met, the contract is registered and assigned a registration number.

4. Transaction performance

Payments are made and goods are delivered, works are performed or services are provided in accordance with the contract.

5. Currency control

Performance and the documents confirming the movement of funds, goods or other contractual performance are accounted for.

6. Completion

Once the obligations have been performed, the applicable currency-control procedures and removal of the contract from registration are addressed.

BEFORE PAYMENT

What should be checked before paying a foreign supplier?

Currency control should not be separated from the legal structure of the foreign trade contract. Before funds are transferred, the transaction should be reviewed as a whole.

  • the parties and the foreign counterparty's details;
  • the subject matter and description of the goods, works or services;
  • total contract value and currency of the obligations;
  • payment terms and payment stages;
  • advance payments and the procedure for recovering or refunding an advance;
  • delivery dates and deadlines for performance by the non-resident;
  • Incoterms and allocation of responsibilities between the parties;
  • documents confirming delivery or other performance;
  • provisions governing changes to deadlines and contract value;
  • governing law and dispute resolution provisions.
LEGAL RISKS

Where do currency-control problems commonly arise?

Payment made, goods not delivered

This may create both a contractual issue involving recovery of funds from the foreign supplier and a need to document the circumstances for currency-control purposes.

Delivery deadline changed

An informal agreement with the supplier does not necessarily replace proper amendment of the written contract. Changes should be documented appropriately.

Contract value changed

Changes to price, quantity or specifications may affect not only the parties' commercial arrangements but also banking and customs documentation.

Transaction documents are inconsistent

The contract, invoice, specifications, payment documents and documents relating to the goods should be reviewed as one integrated transaction file.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Currency Control

When is a foreign trade contract subject to registration?

An import or export currency contract is subject to registration where its amount exceeds the equivalent of USD 50,000. If the contract amount is not specified when the contract is entered into, the contract is also treated as subject to registration.

What is the registration number of a currency contract?

It is an identification number used for accounting and currency-control purposes in relation to a specific import or export currency contract.

When should the registration number be obtained?

If the contract is subject to registration, the registration issue should be addressed before performance of obligations under the currency contract begins.

What if the foreign supplier delays delivery?

The contractual deadlines, correspondence, reasons for the delay and transaction documents should be reviewed. Where the delivery period needs to be changed, the amendment should be properly documented and its currency-control implications assessed.

Should currency-control provisions be reviewed before signing?

A pre-signing legal review can help align contractual deadlines, payment provisions and supporting documents with the actual transaction structure before performance begins.
