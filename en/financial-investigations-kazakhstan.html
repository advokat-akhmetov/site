The Business Owner's Problem

Money Is Missing, but Is It an Accounting Error or Possible Misappropriation?

Financial irregularities in a company are not always immediately apparent. A problem may begin with a minor inventory shortage, a discrepancy between a bank statement and the accounting ledger, an unexplained write-off, or a missing supporting document.

Management may ask the accounting department for explanations, order an inventory count or engage an external auditor. However, where there is a risk that documents or electronic records could be deliberately altered, evidence preservation should be considered at the same time.

Objectivity is essential. A shortage may result from an accounting mistake, inadequate internal controls, a commercial dispute or unlawful conduct. No employee should be treated as guilty merely because a discrepancy has been found.

Warning Signs Worth Examining

  • Unexplained transfers and payments
  • Missing stock, goods or raw materials
  • Differences between 1C records and bank statements
  • Unusual supplier transactions
  • Missing primary accounting documents
  • Deleted or modified accounting information
  • Discrepancies between physical and recorded inventory

Financial Audits and Legal Strategy

Why a Financial Audit Alone May Not Be Enough

A statutory financial statement audit, an accounting review and an internal investigation into suspected asset misappropriation serve different purposes. If company property may have been unlawfully diverted, it is important to identify which evidence needs immediate protection.

Restricted Access to Documents

During an internal conflict, an external specialist may be denied access to certain documents, accounting systems or records. Such restrictions can limit the completeness of the examination.

Risk of Lost Information

Records may be modified or lost while a review is under way. Electronic databases, transaction logs and documents held in only one location may be particularly vulnerable.

Different Legal Powers

A private auditor does not have the investigative powers of law enforcement authorities. Compulsory collection or seizure of documents in criminal proceedings is governed by applicable procedural law.

Key principle: financial examination and legal protection do not always need to occur sequentially. Where justified, they may proceed in parallel, subject to legal requirements and the rights of employees and other persons.

Initial Response

What Should a Business Owner Do When Misappropriation or Embezzlement Is Suspected?

The response should be systematic rather than emotional: establish the initial facts, preserve relevant materials, assess the legal implications and determine the next steps.

STEP 01

Record the Discrepancies

Identify relevant dates, amounts, transactions, assets and supporting documents.

STEP 02

Preserve Available Evidence

Arrange lawful preservation of accounting databases, backups, bank statements and corporate documents.

STEP 03

Conduct a Preliminary Review

Distinguish possible accounting errors from circumstances that may indicate unlawful conduct.

STEP 04

Choose the Legal Response

Assess whether to conduct further internal inquiries, report to competent authorities or pursue recovery of losses.

Do not alter original records, obtain another person's passwords, access private devices unlawfully or seize property without legal authority. Such actions may violate individual rights and undermine the reliability or admissibility of evidence.

Confidential Internal Investigations

How to Organise an Internal Review Without Creating Additional Risks

Informing the entire workforce about suspected wrongdoing is not always necessary for an initial document review. However, confidentiality must be balanced with lawful access, employment rights, data protection obligations and an objective assessment of the facts.

Organisational Measures

  • Identify authorised participants
  • Define lawful access permissions
  • Limit unnecessary disclosure of sensitive information
  • Record the sources of collected documents
  • Preserve original digital records
  • Avoid premature accusations

Legal Measures

  • Assess indications of possible wrongdoing
  • Determine whether specialists are needed
  • Prepare lawful requests and documentation
  • Evaluate reporting options
  • Consider available asset protection measures
  • Review personal data protection requirements

Important: a confidential investigation does not justify sham employment arrangements, unlawful access to personal information, unauthorised surveillance or the fabrication of evidence.

Digital Evidence

What If Accounting Records Have Been Deleted or Altered in 1C?

Accounting and ERP systems may contain records of transactions, documents, user activity and changes to financial information. Where unauthorised modification is suspected, the company should preserve available sources of information without compromising any subsequent technical examination.

Database Backups

Determine whether archived copies of the accounting database exist and whether they may lawfully be examined.

System Logs

Review available transaction and modification histories, where the relevant software retains such information.

Independent Records

Compare accounting entries with bank statements, contracts, invoices, warehouse records and other independent sources.

Recovery of deleted information cannot be guaranteed. The outcome depends on the system, available backups and actions taken after deletion. Qualified IT specialists or digital forensic experts may be required.

Criminal-Law Remedies

When Can a Company Report Suspected Financial Misconduct to Law Enforcement?

If information suggests a possible criminal offence, the company should assess the appropriate reporting procedure without unnecessary delay. Kazakhstan law should not be interpreted as requiring a completed audit report before every allegation of criminal conduct can be submitted.

Preparing a Report

A report should describe known facts, relevant transactions, dates, amounts and available supporting materials.

Established facts should be distinguished from assumptions. The reporting company should not present an individual's guilt as already established.

Pre-Trial Investigation

Where the legal requirements are met, competent authorities may obtain documents, carry out investigative measures and seize items or electronic media in accordance with criminal procedure.

Private auditors and company investigators do not possess equivalent public enforcement powers.

If the authorities do not register or properly address a report: the relevant decision or inaction should be examined under Kazakhstan's Criminal Procedure Code to determine whether a procedural complaint is appropriate.

Legal Classification

Misappropriation or Embezzlement of Entrusted Property: Article 189 of Kazakhstan's Criminal Code

Businesses regularly entrust property to employees and other authorised persons for storage, accounting, use or disposal. If that property later goes missing, the circumstances of its transfer and disappearance must be examined.

Assessment of potential misappropriation or embezzlement involves consideration of the property's legal status, the basis on which it was entrusted, the relevant person's authority, the movement of assets and other circumstances required by criminal law.

An accounting shortage alone does not prove that a criminal offence has occurred. Bookkeeping errors, documentation problems and other explanations may require objective examination.

  • To whom were they entrusted?

Frequently Asked Questions

Financial Audits, Internal Investigations and Suspected Asset Losses

Is an audit mandatory before reporting suspected embezzlement to the police?

Not in every case. Where information indicates signs of a possible criminal offence, reporting to competent authorities may be considered before a complete financial audit. The required supporting materials depend on the circumstances.

Can an internal investigation be conducted confidentially?

Depending on the circumstances and applicable legal requirements, a company may limit knowledge of an initial review to authorised personnel. Employment rights, lawful access restrictions and data protection rules must still be respected.

What if an auditor is denied access to documents?

The legal basis for access, the parties' authority and lawful methods of recording the refusal should be examined. If circumstances suggest a possible criminal offence, reporting options may also need to be assessed.

What if accounting data has been deleted from 1C?

Preserve available databases and backups, avoid unnecessary changes to original records and consider engaging a qualified IT or digital forensics specialist.

Does missing inventory automatically mean embezzlement?

No. Missing inventory may justify further examination, but it does not by itself establish a criminal offence or an individual's guilt.

What if the police insist on an audit report before registration?

The actual procedural decision or inaction must be identified. The available complaint procedure should then be assessed under Kazakhstan's Criminal Procedure Code. The absence of an audit report is not a universal legal justification for refusing every report.

Can a lawyer work together with a financial specialist?

Yes, where appropriate and lawful. Legal representation and financial examination have different professional functions and may complement one another.

Can foreign investors request a review of financial irregularities?

Foreign investors may seek legal advice about corporate rights, access to records, suspected misconduct and available remedies. Specific powers depend on the investor's legal status and the company's governing documents.
