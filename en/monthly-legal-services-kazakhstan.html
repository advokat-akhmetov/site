Business Challenges

When Does a Company Need Ongoing Legal Counsel?

Businesses often seek legal assistance only after a problem has already arisen. Yet many legal risks can be identified before documents are signed, funds are transferred or litigation begins.

RISK 01

A Supplier Takes an Advance Payment but Fails to Deliver

Your company pays for goods or equipment, but the supplier misses deadlines, changes the terms or stops responding.

Legal response: review payment terms, contractual safeguards and risk allocation before transferring funds.

RISK 02

Contracts Are Signed Without Proper Legal Review

Agreements may lack adequate protections, allocate obligations unfairly or expose the business to unexpected liability.

Legal response: examine key contractual provisions and legal consequences before signing.

RISK 03

There Is No In-House Legal Department

Directors, accountants and managers must make legal decisions without dedicated professional support.

Legal response: engage external legal counsel under an agreed service and communication framework.

RISK 04

A Foreign Investor Cannot Independently Assess Local Risks

Local partners manage operations in Kazakhstan, while the investor may struggle to evaluate documents, obligations and counterparties.

Legal response: obtain an independent legal assessment of significant commercial decisions.

RISK 05

Unpaid Invoices and Commercial Claims Are Accumulating

Accounts receivable continue to grow, limitation periods may become an issue, and supporting evidence remains disorganised.

Legal response: organise pre-litigation claims, review deadlines and prepare a legal position.

RISK 06

Every Legal Issue Requires Finding a New Specialist

Management repeatedly spends time finding advisers, transferring documents and explaining the same business context.

Legal response: establish a consistent working relationship with an external legal adviser.

Business Value

Effective Legal Support Is Not Measured by the Number of Consultations

The value of ongoing legal counsel lies in helping a business make commercial decisions with a clearer understanding of their legal consequences.

Legal support may include identifying risks, reviewing contracts, evaluating claims, preparing documents and developing legal positions.

Ongoing legal support does not eliminate commercial risk or guarantee a particular business outcome. Its purpose is to provide professional legal assessment and assistance within the agreed scope.

In-House Lawyer or Outsourced Legal Counsel?

The right choice depends on your workload. If legal tasks arise every day and require continuous on-site availability, an in-house lawyer may be appropriate. If legal matters arise regularly but do not require a full-time employee, outsourced legal support may be a practical option.

Criteria In-House Lawyer Outsourced Legal Counsel
Engagement model Employment relationship Legal services agreement
Costs Salary and associated employer expenses Agreed professional fees
Scope of work Defined by employment duties Defined by the legal services agreement
Availability Based on the employee's working arrangements Based on agreed communication channels and response times
Litigation Subject to applicable procedural requirements Under a separate or expressly agreed assignment
